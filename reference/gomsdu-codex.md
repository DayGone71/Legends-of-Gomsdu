*A living reference document. Version 1 — compiled from recall, not the original notebook.*

## Core Truths (what every inhabitant of Gomsdu knows from near-birth)

- The Land is Gomsdu, made from/by **Gom** and his brother **Kulkrow**.
- The First War happened. The commonly believed cause: jealousy — Gom could create Life, Kulkrow couldn't.
- The Second War happened later. The commonly believed cause: Kulkrow, in a mindless rage, tried to destroy the entire Planet.
- Kulkrow lost both wars and now lives in the Core.
- *(Both causes are propaganda — see "The True History" and "The Pattern Underneath Both Wars" below. Deliberately withheld from players by default.)*
- The story begins in the **Third Era**.

## Cosmology

### The Sun and the Two Sons
**Yom** — almost always called the **Star Father** in-game — is the being that is essentially the Sun. (Originally "Yod" in the creator's decade-old notebook; "Yom" is the name actually used for in-game Lore.)

A being that is essentially the Sun fell in a doomed love with a Planet, and got too close. The Planet was scorched — no life, water, or atmosphere. In response, the Sun sent its two Sons to the Planet, and kept much greater distance from it afterward.

**Their first physical forms, before either of them was anything else:** the Sun fired a Beam or Ball into the Planet. It detonated just under the surface, then rebounded immediately back out, stopping a few meters clear of the ground. Every bit of light involved in that whole sequence — the send, the impact, the small explosion, the rebound, the stop — condensed into a single, impossibly small **White Orb**, which then simply bounced off the surface for millions of years, held down by nothing but gravity, with no awareness of anything, itself included. Eventually it lost momentum and began discharging outward in beams of its own — the literal origin of Lightning, a physical process running long before there was a "Gom" to call it that. It took millions of years before it knew what it was, and eventually learned to control both its own Body and the Lightning coming out of it.

At the far end of that same original Beam, a second Orb formed — pure Blackness, a literal tear in reality. Instead of pulling anything in, it constantly pushed Lava *out* — material that vanished before ever reaching the ground, as if reality hadn't agreed to accept it yet. This one got its act together faster: only a few hundred thousand years before it learned to move itself, became aware of its surroundings, and learned to control the endless expulsion of Lava from its own Body.

Both Orbs were, and are, described as very tiny. What that actually means is left open — fits a pattern the rest of this world already runs on, where the biggest things keep coming from the smallest ones.

- **Gom** — made of Lightning
- **Kulkrow** — made of Lava

**Worth carrying forward: Kulkrow was first.** Faster to move, faster to notice his surroundings, faster to master what he was pouring out of himself. Given everything that happens afterward — Gom as the deafening shout, Kulkrow as the whisper, Gom running the entire System while Kulkrow ends up fragmented and dormant — that's a real inversion sitting at the very beginning. The imbalance was never a natural state; something flipped, hard, between "Kulkrow wakes up first" and "Gom devours everything." What flipped: see "Sound and Shape, and the Name that never formed," directly below.

### Sound and Shape, and the Name that never formed
Sound and Shape — the whole of Language, spoken and written both — are known to both Gom and Kulkrow from the very beginning. Neither one invented it. It comes from **Yom**, inherited in the Womb, before either brother was anything else. But knowing it and being able to use it are different things: neither can speak. And where Gom can still inscribe Shape — the same channel that later lets him engrave thought directly into the Living Stone's minds — Kulkrow can't manage even that much. Lava spills. Nothing he touches holds a controlled mark.

The Living Stone spontaneously coin **GOMGOM** — Gom's own Name, said twice, echoed back at him by his own Creation, an unprompted claim of unity (see "The True History of the First War," below, and `../language/gomsdu-glyphic-system.json` for the word itself). That's the moment Gom realizes what a Name doing that actually means. He doesn't plan what happens to Kulkrow's Name in advance — he doesn't need to. Gom is, at every scale, a Black Hole: his body is whatever falls inside his Event Horizon, and that isn't a metaphor confined to physics or loyalty — it's simply what he does, structurally, everywhere, on every level. Kulkrow's true Name was never going to be an exception. He has no voice to speak it and no medium that will hold it written, so it was always going to stay unclaimed — and nothing unclaimed stays unclaimed near Gom. The instant GOMGOM shows him what a Name can do, he simply, inevitably, closes his hand around the one Name closest to him that had never gotten the chance to exist yet.

**"Kulkrow" is not a lie replacing a true Name.** There was never a window where a true Name could have formed — not in speech, not in writing, not even in either brother's own head, before the concept of Naming-as-claim existed for Gom to exploit. "Kulkrow" isn't propaganda overwriting something real. It's the only thing that was ever going to occupy that space.

**The likely why is in the shape of the two origins themselves.** Gom's is accumulation — light gathering, condensing, collecting into a point. Kulkrow's is expulsion — a wound constantly giving itself away, output pouring out of an absence. That's not just their origin, it's the entire rest of the cosmology: Gom spends every era after this one gathering — devotion, essence, control, followers, eventually "everything and everyone." Kulkrow spends every era after this one giving pieces of himself away and getting smaller for it — Molten Ones, Rainbow Shards, the Shield itself. One thing that collects, one thing that empties, from the very first physical instant either of them existed.

They cooperated for millions of years.

### Language as the Animating Gate
*A grammatical discovery, not authored prose — surfaced from `../language/gomsdu-glyphic-system.json`'s own oldest rule (a completing chevron marks presence/animation; its absence marks an unanimated, aura-only form — see the Glyphic System's grammar rules) via a worked Composition built in the Glyph Arranger, 2026-08-19, creator-confirmed as deliberate. Full transcription in the JSON's correctionLog.*

KRO and KROW are the very first two Glyphs this language ever had: the same life-vessel container, present versus absent its animating chevron. A Composition written this session put that oldest distinction to real narrative use. Three of Gom's own verbs acting on Life — Create (OG), Teach (WIM), Maintain (KA) — all left the vessel as KRO, uncompleted, regardless of which verb was used. Completion into KROW happened at exactly one point: passing through **GI**, the Word — this language's own name for its smallest complete unit, not incidental vocabulary. Every branch, whatever its origin, had to cross that single gate.

What came after the gate split three ways. Take and Give both left the vessel KROW — completion preserved. Kill did not — and specifically, the Kill branch's actor was **Kulkrow**, not Gom, the only branch where the subject itself changes. Its result reverts the vessel to KRO: not destroyed, unmade back to what it was before it was ever spoken.

**The reading:** none of Gom's actions animate Life by his own hand alone, whatever the verb. Animation is granted at the moment of Naming — of passing through Language itself. And Kulkrow's kill is the precise inverse of that one specific event, nothing more: it doesn't end the vessel, it un-names it.

This sits directly beside "Sound and Shape, and the Name that never formed," above. Language already does something momentous to a Name there — GOMGOM, and what Gom's Black-Hole nature does to Kulkrow's own unclaimed one. This is the same mechanism read at the general case: not just what Language did to one being's Name, but what it does, structurally, to Life itself, every time. Also consonant with WIMBIS's established character ("the thing that teaches you by making you forget") — Language in this cosmology is never neutral. It's the site where completion, or its reversal, actually happens.

*Not yet confirmed as universal, binding canon beyond this one worked Composition — open thread: does this generalize to every act of creation/destruction in the cosmology, or is it a property of this specific Composition and the Gom/Kulkrow/Life triad specifically? Flagged for creator.*

### A Cultural Footnote: Reading Direction
Horizontal mirroring carries no grammatical meaning (see the Glyphic System's own grammar rules) — that hasn't stopped anyone from having opinions about it. Humans, on the East Coast, read right-to-left; Orks, in the West, read left-to-right. Structurally meaningless, culturally loaded enough to start a real fight over — the same "everyone gets a convenient version" pattern the rest of this document runs on at cosmic scale, just small enough here to happen at a dinner table instead of a war. **Sibmiw** — Wimbis's own name, read straight backward — is a known derogatory usage, not a neutral dialectal variant.

### The Founding Rule
Neither brother creates from nothing. Both act on the Planet's own material — Kulkrow shapes and separates **Matter**, Gom infuses **Life/Essence** — which is why they needed each other to make anything at all, and why the *availability* of raw material gates what either of them can do at any point in history. **Liquid, as a category, doesn't exist until Death does.** Neither brother can produce it directly, only what happens as a consequence of it — this holds all the way through both wars.

### Making the Living Stone
The Planet was originally **solid and singular** — individual existence wasn't possible at all until Kulkrow began melting pieces off it, one at a time. Gom could then infuse a piece with life *while it was still molten*, and it would cool into something solid and living: the **Living Stone**.

Important asymmetry: the Living Stone valued their **Essence** (Gom's gift) far more than their **Form** (Kulkrow's gift). They came to recognize only Gom as their Creator. Gom was also present *within their thinking* in a way Kulkrow never was.

**Living Stone are like limbs of Gom** — permanently connected, no pain in the bond, but no true independence either, even though it feels that way to them.

### The True History of the First War
**Secret. Only Gom and Kulkrow currently know this. Not common knowledge, not written anywhere, not held by any faction — buried deep by design, meant to be earned in play rather than found.**

Gom recognized his unique hold on the Living Stone's loyalty and their minds, and used it to oppress his brother and exploit his labor.

Kulkrow, in secret, spent millennia trying to create life of his own — the **Molten Ones** — using pure lava, without the piece of the process only Gom could provide. The tragedy: **Death did not exist before this.** His creations could only live briefly — first seconds, then (as he refined the craft over millennia) minutes, hours, days. Never permanent, unlike the Living Stone. **Molten Ones are continuously-sustained extensions of Kulkrow himself** — kept alive only by him pouring himself into them, at agony comparable to being partially skinned alive or losing a finger, over and over. The moment he withdraws, they collapse.

When Gom and the Living Stone discovered what Kulkrow had been secretly crafting, the **First War** began. There are no weapons — Living Stone and Molten Ones fight by contact. When they touch, they fuse, melt together, and their combined remains become **Water** — the first Liquid to ever exist, carrying elements of both brothers. To Kulkrow, every such death is permanent, agonizing separation. To Gom, touching Water directly is worse than losing a Stone — he loses the ability to hold himself together at all, instead feeling all Water everywhere at once.

A war of attrition: Gom can't make more Living Stone — that needed Kulkrow's cooperation, and cooperation ended when the war started — so every loss is permanent. Kulkrow's only real limit is how much Matter remains on the Planet to shape.

With only a few Living Stone left, Gom does something desperate: he infuses himself directly into a Molten One. It explodes, scattering across the Planet as the **Rainbow Shards** — both brothers fused into solid form, far more intensely than the diffuse mixing in Water. Solid versus Liquid.

It's too much for Kulkrow. He recedes into the Earth, tunneling as deep as he can. He discovers Water solidifies near him — letting him "repair" the Planet, though he doesn't use this yet — and that he can boil it, creating the Planet's first atmosphere. Gom retreats to whatever dry land exists, avoiding Kulkrow's occasional rainstorms. Gom is the first to notice that Water plus Matter, given enough time, becomes **Mud** — letting him make Abominations of his own, mirroring the Molten Ones.

*(The "jealousy and sorcery" story believed in the present day flattens all of this into something living beings can hold onto — the same instinct that lets the story of Gom weeping the Flood into being persist uncorrected, even though neither brother can actually cry. Liquid only exists because Death does.)*

### The Second War: Betrayal at the Crystal Mountains
Gom discovers Rainbow Shards can be transformed — subjecting one to continuous Lightning Storms (thousands of strikes, spread over hundreds of days) turns it into **Crystal**. He can infuse Crystal the way he once infused Living Stone. The problem: Kulkrow isn't merely shaped by a Rainbow Shard, he's *part of it*. Turning one into a Crystal Being tortures him directly — nerve endings exposed to fire that never burn away or go numb, sustained. Unimaginable, ongoing pain, on a completely different scale than the Molten Ones.

Kulkrow doesn't know at first that his growing pain is Gom's doing. Eventually he surfaces — deliberately, this time — to where Gom lives, and finds two entire civilizations: Mud Creatures walking the land, and Crystal spires holding Crystal Beings, eerily reminiscent of the old Living Stone. They talk. The old animosity seems behind them. Kulkrow asks why he hurts. Gom knows exactly why, and doesn't say — he answers with something true enough to satisfy the question without ever naming the cause, then redirects: suggesting they build the Crystal City together. Kulkrow agrees, sacrificing himself over and over: making fresh Molten Ones so Gom can destroy them and harvest more Rainbow Shards.

Kulkrow weakens as the mountains rise. At the pinnacle of the highest peak, Gom builds a Chamber and seals himself inside — and Kulkrow is hit with a horrifying realization: existence is now mostly a fusion of the two of them, Gom as strong as the day he arrived, Kulkrow severely diminished, and the sensation building in him is Gom trying to take over his body and mind. **Gom's real gamble:** knowing Crystallization was destroying his brother, he bet that diminishing Kulkrow while becoming Master of everything Kulkrow was invested in would give him a backdoor into total control — the same control he already had over every other Created Being.

It doesn't quite work. Kulkrow accidentally destroys a Crystal Being and, for the first time in his existence, feels relief — the part of himself trapped inside returns to him. Enraged, he decides to convert *all* remaining Planetary Matter into a single Molten One and use it to destroy all Life — becoming whole again, and ending Gom's schemes for good.

He's too weak. Gom keeps infusing himself into the melting Planet as it breaks apart; most chunks become inert Stone, a few become new Rainbow Shards, and what Gom manages to infuse gets consumed almost immediately by the surrounding molten mass, creating more Water. By the end, all remaining Matter has become Water. The Second War is far shorter than the First. Virtually nothing solid is left: the Core, the Crystal Mountains and a little land around them, and an endless ocean.

Kulkrow, stretched past his limit, starts falling apart into Goo. Gom and the Crystal Beings realize the Goo has a use: roughly a teaspoon of it in a hundred gallons of Water yields a hundred gallons (and a teaspoon) of **Blood** — a medium Gom can actually exist within, unlike the chaos of touching Water directly. He begins drowning the Mud Beings in Blood, weaving Veins, building the first Hearts — the **Progenitors**, the first true Organic Beings, with Gom living inside the Blood itself.

Facing only two options — back off, or destroy everything — Kulkrow makes one more radical move: forcing his entire remaining Being into a small piece of Matter, becoming the **Shield**. It spares him most of the pain of separation, but leaves him almost powerless, a single dense, solid thing. Seeing his chance to finish the takeover, Gom attacks the Shield directly. The attack fails, reflecting back on Gom, and the Shield shatters into 4 pieces of **Black Stone** — the only colorless material on the Planet. Denied even that, Kulkrow becomes a Black Cloud wrapped around a small ball of raging fire and descends into the Core one final time, forming **the Pit**.

The only Matter left transforms into Mud — a floating island on a planet-wide Ocean, tethered to the Core through the Pit. Kulkrow carves a Tunnel and a Shell, solidifying just enough Water to make both, and goes dormant. Gom, the Crystal Beings, and the Progenitors gather what drops of Kulkrow's Goo they can find, building reservoirs of Blood, and water the Mud Island with it — producing Roots, a new form of Life. **Gomsdu is born.**

Thousands of years pass before the game begins. What's remembered, broadly: Kulkrow once tried to destroy the whole Planet in a rage.

### The Pattern Underneath Both Wars
Neither war's official story is false, exactly — each is just missing everything that would make Gom look like the instigator. "Jealousy and sorcery" omits the exploitation that started it. "Kulkrow's mindless rage" omits the centuries of secret torture, the lie about its source, and the attempted hostile takeover that provoked it. Every era gets a version simple enough to hold as morality — Gom good, Kulkrow bad — and every version happens to protect the same person. **This is a repeating structural feature of Gom's character, not a one-off deception.**

## Blood

- Blood is Kulkrow's dissolving substance (Goo) diluted into Water — roughly a teaspoon of Goo per hundred gallons of Water. Every living thing with Blood in it carries a literal trace of him.
- It's the medium Gom actually exists *within*, unlike Water, which is chaos to him. The first Organic Beings (the Progenitors) were created by drowning Mud Beings in it and weaving the first Hearts and Veins.
- Likely explanation for why Trees and Rocks bleed, in myth: Water made whatever dormant spark already existed in all Matter (from the original Kulkrow-separation / Gom-infusion process) expressible for the first time — not deliberate design, a side effect nobody planned. *(Close to confirmed; full mechanic still being finalized.)*
- **Gom can be understood, at core, as blood-drunk.** He is everything and everyone; all created life is a buffet to him, each creature a new way to taste the same "ruby nectar."
- **Every contemporary race is defined by its relation to Blood.** Humans are done — see The Society, under Factions. The Human Queen isn't an echo of Gom's nature, she's a direct, mortal-scale expression of it — arguably the purest one that exists at human scale. Other races' relations are known to the creator but not yet documented — active thread, see Open Questions Log.

## The Present Day (Third Era)

Kulkrow is fragmented — mostly a raging mass of lava at the Core rather than anything with a Form. His **Shield** — Black Stone, the only colorless material on the Planet, born from his last desperate act at the end of the Second War — exists in **4 pieces**. If reassembled, he could take physical Form on the surface.

**Whether that's good news or catastrophic is deliberately unresolved.** Design intent: the party's own choices and relationship with Kulkrow's side determine how this reads, not a fixed "correct" answer.

The true history is likely surfaced late — plausibly Kulkrow himself telling someone, for the first time ever, once the party has earned real Loyalty with his side. That reveal and the choice of whether to help him regain Form are meant to land as close to the same moment as possible.

## Geography

*A schematic map exists as `gomsdu-map.svg` alongside this document. Placeholder race names below (Elf, Dwarf, Ork, Hobbit) are shorthand for storytelling role, not a commitment to standard fantasy conventions — chosen for narrative utility, not art direction.*

### The North (three areas)
- **Crystal Mountains** — mostly crystal except the range's southern end; home to the Living Stone's descendants, who watch over the land. The range's foot reaches south toward the West, where the Dwarves claim its non-crystal tail.
- **The Forest** — the Main City of the Elf-like beings sits deep in the wood, built up in foothills to its west. The deep, dark forest to its east is hallowed ground *(now explained — see The Elves, under Factions)*.
- **The Tundra** — the forest thins going east and fades into tundra. **World's End**, in the far northeast corner, is home to Hobbit-like folk.

### Human Lands (the middle)
Directly south of the Forest, taking up most of the middle of the continent. A massive wall separates it from the Forest to the north.
- Western border: past the foothills, curving around the mountains, south a ways, then back east until it hits the ocean.
- Eastern border: stays adjacent to forest for a while, then fades into the Tundra going east.
- **Bane** — the established starting location — is a coastal city on the western shore.

### The Peninsula (south)
South of Human Lands. Desert, with giant monsters living in the sand that make the mountains at the tip — **the Pit** — nearly impossible to reach by land. **The mountain range itself is the fallen body of the Melting Titan** — the Being that Kulkrow's Second War attempt to convert all remaining Matter into one final Molten One actually produced (see Cosmology). It fell here, curling around the Pit as if to shield Kulkrow during his final descent — which is why the range rings the land the way it does, rather than following any ordinary geological logic.

### The West (hills)
Very hilly, bordered by the Human wall to the east.
- **North Hills** — Dwarves, at the foot of the Crystal Mountain range; claim a few of its southern peaks, made of a different material than the rest of the range (not crystal). One of their strongholds sits exactly where the Human Wall ends and the Elven forest stops.
- **The Port** — a massive port on the western shore, center of Ork civilization; Orks populate most of the hills.
- **Mounds & Nomads** — the rest of the hills: Mound-dwellers and Nomadic tribes.
- Southern border shares part of the desert with the Peninsula — that stretch is monster-free, but home to the harshest tribes *(the Sandmen — see The Orks, the Port, and the Nomads, under Factions)*.

*Open: the other 3 starting locations still aren't placed, deliberately — but candidate regions now exist (the Forest, Crystal Mountains, and West Hills are all plausible fits) once it's time to decide.*

## Factions

### The Living Stone & the Crystal Beings
- **True Living Stone** — First and Second Generation, made during and around the First War, miraculous survivors of both wars — still exist, hidden deep *under* the Crystal Mountains, apart from the surface population entirely.
- **Crystal Beings** — the population that actually lives across the Crystal Mountains today, and what "Living Stone descendants" usually refers to in play. "Descendants" means spiritual successors, not unbroken lineage — they're Gom's Second War creation, made by Crystallizing Rainbow Shards, not a continuation of the originals.
- Crystal Beings have no Hearts, no real possibility of understanding what they are, and are completely bound to Gom's Will — **except for one sliver**: the slightest capability for self-recognition. Nothing in the lore suggests they know this about themselves. The main storyline treats it as real but very obscure, reachable only if a Crystal Being experiences its own destruction — it has to melt and soften before the potential becomes real.
- **This is the actual mechanic behind Loyalty for this faction:** the only way to give a Crystal Being a chance at a different loyalty is to kill it.
- They don't know the true history of either war, or that Kulkrow is fused into their own substance.

### Kulkrow's Forces (Molten Ones / Cultists)
- Not necessarily uniform — Kulkrow refined his craft over millennia, so different "generations" plausibly have different lifespans/stability (early ones: seconds; later ones: days). Unexplored design space.
- Currently seeking to reunite the 4 Shield Fragments.
- **Recruitable via a Loyalty mechanic**, distinct from the Living Stone's fixed devotion — their cause has real grief behind it, even if the wider world doesn't know it.

### The Society (the Human Queendom)
- Extremely authoritarian. Ruled by a Queen — placeholder name **Kali** — over the entire Queendom.
- Her subjects are collectively considered her servants, and literally her diet. She eats her own people.
- Devotion is the actual social order: the most loyal (thousands of them) compete in daily gladiatorial combat for the honor of being eaten by her.
- Devotion intensity scales with proximity to the Capital. Further out, more places try to ignore she exists at all — though her Army controls the whole Queendom regardless of local sentiment.
- **The Human Wall exists mainly to keep her subjects in**, not to keep outsiders out.
- A major highway connects the important cities; the land between is farms and small towns.
- *Thematic note, revised: not an echo — a direct expression. Gom is everything and everyone, fundamentally blood-drunk; all created life is a buffet, each being a new way to taste the same "ruby nectar." A queen who's built her entire society around being fed her own worshippers isn't paralleling his nature from a distance — she's one of its purest expressions at human scale. See Blood, above.*
- **She may literally be Gom, in the Flesh.** "Lightning made Flesh" is the phrase everyone — cultists, ordinary Gomites, even Gom himself — uses to describe what she is, in terms anyone can understand. Whether that's the literal truth is unknown to anyone, Gom included (see "What Nobody Knows," below).
- She's ruled for roughly 900 years.

### What Nobody Knows (Not Even Gom)
Two questions sit permanently outside the reach of any answer in this setting — not undecided, deliberately unknowable:
- **What the Sun actually was**, before it loved a Planet and lost itself doing it. Forgotten even by Gom and Kulkrow (see Cosmology) — remembered only by the Sandmen (see below).
- **Whether the Queen is literally Gom in the Flesh**, or simply the closest a mortal has ever come to being him. *(Resolved for design purposes only — see "The True Union," reserved endgame material. Stays unknown in-world and to players outside the true ending.)*

These aren't gaps waiting to be filled. They're meant to stay open — one permanently, one until the ending earns it.

### The Dwarves
- Sit on more Blood than any other race, by a wide margin — the Reservoirs lie beneath their Mountain homes.
- Where other races are consumed by Blood-hunger, Dwarves barely seem to notice it exists. Abundance, not indifference — when something is simply always there, it stops registering as an obsession.
- Their culture is built around **stewardship** of the Reservoirs: physical proximity to the Blood is status. Someone who actually scoops it holds one of the highest positions in Dwarven society. They drink it, wash in it, and run Blood-fueled machinery no other race has.
- Other races know about the machines and the fuel — they just don't know the fuel is Blood, since Dwarves alter it (changing its color, at minimum) before it ever leaves their hands.
- Public face to outsiders: cold, removed, motives unclear — including a puzzling habit of seeming more interested in other races' junk than anything genuinely valuable. (Makes sense from the inside — they're not short on anything that actually matters to them.)

**The Chosen**
- Some Dwarves develop Red Eyes with a small pupil — the same condition as the Men of Gom: fully possessed (see "Possession: the Two Voices"). Once it happens, the rest of Dwarven society makes exceptions for their behavior, since they're said to "walk with Gom."
- Chosen periodically form Bands of roughly 100 and leave the Mountain Cities, never seen or heard from again.
- What actually happens: Gom leads them to a place of his choosing, forces them to lose a fight against ordinary wildlife, and they die — scattering all the Blood they carried into a new area of Gomsdu. In the most desolate locations, there's no wildlife to do the killing, so the Chosen are forced to tear each other apart with their bare hands instead.
- A fully possessed being stays completely conscious throughout — mind aware, body and voice entirely controlled by Gom. Every Chosen Dwarf believes, to the last, that they're walking with Gom in the highest honor their culture has. What they're actually experiencing is being forced to lose, and in the worst cases forced to kill people they marched out with, fully aware of it happening and unable to do anything at all.
- Dwarven society has no idea any of this happens. No remains, no witnesses capable of speech — wolves don't talk, and the only people who could ever explain it are dead. Not a kept secret — a horror with no path to ever being discovered by normal means.

**Blood framing so far:** Humans feast on it. Dwarves steward it, while a horror neither they nor anyone else could ever detect runs underneath that stewardship.

### The Elves
- Essentially immortal — no natural aging death. Their entire existence orbits shaping what Blood can do, through the Forest: growing it, guiding it, building with it. Differs from Dwarven stewardship in kind, not just degree — Dwarves guard a fixed store; Elves actively cultivate and grow it into new living forms, and ultimately return their own bodies into the cycle at the end.
- Hatched from massive Seed-Pods, not born. Most of an Elf's life is spent learning to grow Plant Life. Their buildings are reworked living Trees; their clothing is harvested living material; the Forest works *with* them rather than being merely shaped by them.
- **The most Essence-filled of all races, and the lore-confirmed answer to hybrid casting** — they mix both schools deliberately: Lava to shape the Forest's physical features, Lightning to guide its behavior. Precisely the established domain split (Lava = matter/form, Lightning = life), used in concert rather than picking one.
- Progression is exposure to increasingly dangerous parts of the Woods — Rites of Passage are pure combat prowess, since the Woods actively try to kill them. Population stays small because, eventually, every Elf dies there.
- The greatest among them eventually clear even the deepest, darkest part of the Forest and reach the **Massive Tree** — free, at that point, to do anything they want in Elven society, having exhausted every threat the Woods could offer. When they tire of existing, they retire there instead: dig a small grave, lie down, and let the Roots cover them. The Roots destroy them completely — like being wrung out like wet laundry, or fed through a juicer.
- **This is why the deep dark forest east of the Elf city reads as hallowed ground** (see Geography) — it's where their culture's whole life cycle actually completes.
- **Gom's role here is different from anywhere else: he's only an Observer.** Their culture is already built around self-destruction, so he never needs to take control — he just cultivates continual awareness in them instead, which is exactly why they reach Essence levels no other race can touch. He knows, with total certainty, that they'll eventually feed themselves back into the System on their own, believing their voluntary end is the height of personal mastery — an air of extreme elitism, without any comprehension of what they're actually perpetuating.

**Blood framing so far:** Humans feast on it. Dwarves guard it. Elves grow it, and eventually grow into it.

### The Orks, the Port, and the Nomads
- Orks and Nomads share the Hills — Orks build Mounds to live in, Nomads live more exposed. The Port City itself is a genuine melting pot; unlike everywhere else on the map, no single race controls it.
- The Hills are one of the only places on Gomsdu with regular Rain, and so one of the few places with real excess Water. Water and Blood work together everywhere, but Blood dominates culture and discussion — Water is mostly taken for granted, necessary but background, the way water on Earth is necessary without being culturally central.
- **Fungi are the one confirmed exception to Gomsdu's Blood-based biology** — mostly Water, not Blood. Blood shows up on them only externally, as a thin surface film (like a clot or vascular coating), present only when the soil provides it — a fungus grown in Blood-poor soil just has a clear surface instead.
- **The Blood Film is what the Orks live for.** They eat it, raw or cooked, and coat their possessions in it. Removed from the fungus, it doesn't die — it keeps writhing and living independently, though it doesn't really move; more like thick, living moss.
- Growing the Film requires Blood-soaked soil. **This is the actual root of Ork violence** — not aggression for its own sake, but agriculture. If the soil reads too dry, the response is simple: start a war with each other to improve conditions. Warfare *is* farming, for them.
- **The Nomads** are drawn from every other race — people who reject their home society and choose isolation instead. The Hills are the only place that's actually viable for this, and only because **Gom lets them live there**: in the Hills, they're perpetually hunted by Orks, who want them specifically because they're ideal fertilizer. Rejecting the Blood-system doesn't mean escaping it — it means being funneled into a different feeding mechanism instead.
- There's a mechanical reason Gom tolerates the arrangement: Water is dangerous to him — he loses himself in it (see Cosmology) — and the Hills' near-constant Rain makes them a place he, and the Men of Gom, struggle to reach directly. Nomads who flee there are, in effect, fleeing to the one place beyond his easy grasp. The Orks hunting them down closes that gap for him without him having to do anything himself.
- One specific tribe, the **Sandmen**, lives in the drier southwestern Hills, right where they border the desert — the harshest of the Nomad tribes, and the ones already noted holding that stretch of monster-free desert (see Geography). **The shared name is deliberate, not a collision.** To virtually everyone, they read as exactly what the name implies — tough desert people, wrapped in cloth against the sun. What they actually are is some of the rarest lore in the game (see The Sandmen, under Factions).
- Neat tidbit: the Hills are the greenest, most verdant land on Gomsdu — a direct consequence of the Rain.

**Blood framing so far:** Humans feast on it. Dwarves guard it. Elves grow it, and eventually grow into it. Orks farm it, through war. Nomads flee it, and become fertilizer for someone else's harvest instead.

### The Pattern, Once More — Now at the Racial Scale
Three races, three completely different cultural shapes, hiding the exact same exploitation. Humans don't hide it at all — devotion-through-consumption is celebrated openly, no illusion required. Dwarves are kept in total, structural ignorance — an atrocity with literally no possible witness. Elves deceive *themselves*, proudly — their most sacred rite reads internally as personal mastery and elite completion, when it's actually another way of feeding the same System, and Gom doesn't even need to intervene, because their own culture does the concealing for him. **The Nomads add a fourth variant: the illusion of opting out.** Rejecting a home society's version of the System doesn't grant freedom from it — it just changes which mechanism eventually collects you. Same underlying hunger every time, dressed differently enough that none of them recognize it in the others, let alone in themselves. Worth watching for as more races get added.

### The Hobbits (World's End)
- Live in the Tundra — the only place on Gomsdu where Snow exists. Even the Crystal Mountains stay snow-free, since Crystal Beings actively keep moisture from reaching their domain.
- Snow is frozen Water, and freezing it doesn't just make it dangerous to Gom the way ordinary Water is — it makes it somewhere he genuinely cannot reach at all.
- The terrain itself is what isolates them — a literal god-being simply cannot get near. They have no idea this is why; they're just content, living out their lives in their one small City (a Town, by any other race's standard).
- Their Blood runs different, from the cold. Gom is technically still in them — everyone carries him, per "Possession: the Two Voices" — but reduced so far past his normal presence that he might as well not be there at all. **Hobbits are the one race where Gom's presence drops to what Kulkrow's normally is everywhere else** — barely a whisper. Even high-Essence creatures struggle in the Tundra; the cold impedes the connection and runs their Blood cold along with it.

### The Sandmen
- Live in near-pure desert sand, wrapped head to toe in tight cloth — the wrapping isn't decoration, it's what keeps them a single entity.
- **The only Lifeform with no Blood in them at all.** Not reduced, not diluted — absent. They are pure Sand, nothing else.
- Disconnected from both Brothers entirely: Gom wants nothing to do with them, and Kulkrow doesn't even know they exist.
- Origin: not all of Gomsdu's matter became Mud at the end of the Second War. A small layer was superheated instead, becoming Grains of Sand — the purest matter left on the Planet. Purer even than the Stone Kulkrow made by solidifying Water, since that Stone was still removed from the Planet, shaped into two different Forms of Life, and eventually melted down. The Sand is the only Matter that was never directly touched by either Brother, through any of it.
- Because neither Brother ever reached it, something else did: **the Planet itself came alive**, and organized the Sand into Living Creatures on its own.
- That total independence is exactly why the Sandmen are the most aware of all Life on Gomsdu — **they're the only ones who still remember what the Sun actually is. Gom and Kulkrow have forgotten where they came from.** The single largest secret left in the cosmology belongs to the race everyone else treats as scenery.
- They spend their days resting, buried still enough in the sand that travelers walk over them without noticing. At night they rise and stare at the stars — longing for a distant light neither of the brothers who made this world can even remember anymore.

### The Boundary of the System
Every race so far has been caught somewhere inside Gom's design, in a different disguise each time. The Hobbits and Sandmen are the exception that defines the boundary rather than another variant hiding inside it — proof that being outside the System is possible, but only under conditions extreme enough that almost nothing else on Gomsdu meets them: total physical isolation by frozen Water on one side, total purity from ever being touched by either Brother on the other. The Sandmen's isolation turns out to matter more than anyone in-world could guess — they're the only ones left who remember what started all of this in the first place.

**Example inciting incident (Bane start):** A group of Kulkrow Cultists has found one of the 4 Fragments in the Prison City of Bane and is trying to burn the city down to escape with it.

## The Player-Facing Reveal: A Process
The world should read as beautiful and genuinely loved on the surface — no wink, no tell. Uncovering the Blood Economy is meant to produce a real, gut-level sick feeling in the *player*, not just their character: the inversion is that being completely cherished by Gomsdu and being quietly harvested by it are, from inside, the same experience.

**The sequence, in order:**
1. **Wonder, played straight.** Nothing sinister yet, because nothing is lying to the player yet.
2. **Blood as charm, not dread.** Contraptions, customs, living architecture — texture the player enjoys before they know what it's built from.
3. **One small, personal crack.** A specific image, not a lecture — a named NPC, not a system explained aloud.
4. **It happened to them.** The Man of Gom / Lightning-agency trade is the strongest lever available, because theme and mechanic are the same event — a player who invested in Lightning has been trading away control the whole time, and realizing that recontextualizes their own choices, not just the lore.
5. **The wrong-but-plausible explanation, planted first.** Every horror the player finds should have an existing, reasonable theory blaming Kulkrow or Gom's *absence* rather than his design — Water genuinely weakening Gom's reach (see The Orks, the Port, and the Nomads) is the ready-made mechanism for this. Sympathy should land on Gom initially.
6. **Unraveling the redirect, not the horror itself, is the real climax.** The gut-punch isn't "Gom did something bad" — it's "I already forgave him for this once, using a fact he made sure was true and convenient." Deception squared: it indicts the player's own reasoning, not just their trust.
7. **An earned choice, not a delivered one.** By the time the true First/Second War history surfaces, a player who's done the work has already lived this exact pattern once at smaller scale — so the cosmic reveal confirms a suspicion rather than dropping a twist, and "help Kulkrow or not" becomes a real question instead of an obvious one.

## The Prophecy of Krowkist
*The concrete, in-play version of the "wrong but plausible" mechanism described under "The Player-Facing Reveal: A Process."*

Kulkrow wants his Blood back. That's the real motivating force behind the cults built around him — not malice, not simple revenge, a physical and metaphysical restoration.

**The Prophecy, in sequence:**
1. The Orks fight the Monsters in the Desert.
2. The Nomads pave the way after them.
3. The Dwarves pour their Blood into the Pit.
4. The Elven Tree gets up and walks into the Pit.
5. The Crystal Beings migrate onto the Melting Titan's remains in the Southern Peninsula (see Geography) — commonly believed to produce something called **Blood Crystal**. What that would actually be is intentionally left open to endless in-world speculation; the game doesn't need to answer it, since it sits well beyond almost any realistic end-game scope.
6. The Queen stands at the edge of the Pit.
7. Kulkrow ascends.

**What "the Union" actually means depends entirely on who's asked — and this is what resolves how the Prophecy relates to the existing Shield quest, rather than sitting apart from it:**
- **The mainstream understanding** — held by virtually all Cultists *and* ordinary Gomites (people generally aware Gom exists), not a fringe belief — is simply that the Queen hands the reassembled Shield to the Cloud (Kulkrow). That's it. Kulkrow is Reborn. **This is the existing Shield-fragment quest itself**, understood religiously, requiring the Queen specifically to be the one who delivers it. "All Matter would cease to Be" is a disputed folk consequence attached to this widely-known event — something everyone's heard and argues about, not a secret.
- **Group S** holds exactly this mainstream reading, and is by far the most influential Krowkist variant.
- **Group SSS** holds a version almost nobody knows exists: one additional step beyond the Shield handover — Kulkrow (Form, newly Reborn) and the Queen (Essence) must physically kiss, combine directly, to complete what they call the "True Union."
- **Nearly everyone informed — cultists and Gomites alike — believes the Queen is the only truly Living Creature in existence, "the Alive One," and therefore the only Being capable of genuinely holding the Gomhead.** This is why she matters to every version of the Prophecy regardless of which one turns out to be right, and it places her well past "political antagonist" — she may be the one mortal in Gomsdu theologically positioned to actually *become* Gom outright, not merely be possessed by him the way a Man of Gom is.

**Why sympathy for Gom emerges from this, specifically:** once a player notices how much of Gomsdu revolves around Blood, "Gom is trying to prevent this" is a completely reasonable conclusion, built entirely on true facts — Kulkrow really did try to destroy all life once; controlling Blood everywhere really would slow anyone trying to reassemble him. The twist isn't that the conclusion is false. It's that it's unnecessary as an explanation — Gom would behave exactly this way even without the Prophecy, because it's simply what he is. "Stopping an apocalypse" is a convenient story for behavior he'd have anyway.

### The Cultists Fragment — Reflecting Kulkrow Himself
Kulkrow's followers split into distinct, often mutually hostile groups rather than one unified movement — a fair reflection of what Kulkrow himself actually is: fragmented, contradictory, not a single coherent will.

- **Group A — Accessible.** Focused on raising awareness of the Blood Economy, and correctly identify Gom as the one keeping it running. Their theory: if Kulkrow returns, there's a real chance of ending it for good. The most genuinely sympathetic faction — grief-driven, not hate-driven, and closest to actually being right about something.
- **Group M — Maniacs.** No real ideology beyond a muttered creed: *"As long as Blood is being spilt, Kulkrow will Bless the Hand."* Violence as devotion, not a means to any particular end.
- **Group S — Sacred.** Believe the Queen — "the Alive One," Lightning made Flesh, uniquely fit for "conscious assumption of the Gomhead" — must preside over the Union of Cloud (Kulkrow) and Shield. If she does, all Matter ceases to Be. A genuinely apocalyptic reading: the Union ends everything.
- **Group SSS — Super Secret Sacrilege.** A heretical splinter of Group S's belief: the Queen must kiss the Reborn Kulkrow directly, so Form and Essence can complete a "True Union" — not annihilation, but a literal reenactment of the process that first made the Living Stone, at cosmic scale, with consequences distinct from what Group S expects.
- **Group Z — Zealots.** Reject total annihilation outright — believe whoever survives the Union ascends to live forever in the (now) Blood Crystal Mountains, eternally preying on the Northern Lands past the desert. Emotionally hate-driven rather than grief-driven — an "Accessible Extremist" position: same opening complaint as Group A, but the desired outcome is personal power and domination rather than liberation.

**The moral framing mechanism, made explicit:** because Groups S, SSS, and Z all point toward outcomes that read, by any reasonable measure, as genuinely catastrophic — annihilation, or eternal predatory conquest — opposing the Prophecy reads as the conventional light-side choice honestly, through ordinary genre convention, no withheld information required. Those outcomes really are worth opposing. The deception sits one level deeper: a correct, well-earned tactical call ("stop the apocalypse cult") gets silently laundered by the player's own reasoning into a much larger, unearned conclusion ("therefore Gom, and the System he runs, must be good").

**Temporal framing, worth writing consistently:** the cultists think in endgame terms — one climactic Union that resolves everything. Gom thinks in centuries. That contrast should be audible in how each side is written — the cultists urgent and apocalyptic, Gom patient and procedural, playing a far longer game than anyone chasing the Prophecy realizes.

**Kulkrow himself has no idea any of this exists.** Dormant in the Core for thousands of years, with no established way to perceive the surface, he's as cut off from the Prophecy built in his name as everyone up there is cut off from him. What little he still does — solidifying Water, boiling it into rain — is likely unconscious, the way a sleeping body still breathes: entire arrangements up top (the Hills' rain, the Ork/Nomad ecosystem built around it) may be shaped by involuntary processes he's never once been aware of causing. *(Open: whether that obliviousness is total and permanent, or whether anything could eventually crack it — matters for how reachable he is in any endgame confrontation.)*

**Resolved:** the Queen's role in the Prophecy is no longer a question of willing-versus-coerced — see "What Nobody Knows," under Factions.

## The True Union — Endgame Material (Reserved)
*A hard limit, deliberately: this sits beyond the lore, mechanics, and narrative that make up the rest of the game. Everything else in this document exists to earn this moment, not to explain it in advance. Should stay unknown in-world and to players outside the true ending — see "What Nobody Knows," under Factions, which this resolves for design purposes only.*

Both Krowkist readings of the Union — Group S's Shield-handover, Group SSS's kiss — are wrong. So is everyone's assumption that Kulkrow is central to it.

**What's actually true:** at the instant the Shield shattered at the end of the Second War, something was created by accident — Gom's Life-essence meeting Kulkrow's most concentrated, purest Matter, the same combination that deliberately made the Living Stone, this time happening in violence, unintended by either brother. In that same instant, **Gom's individual Form vanished from Reality**, and — for him specifically, and for anything he ever put himself into — time began running backward. The "Gom" narrated throughout the rest of this document, patient, long-game, orchestrating the Blood Economy across millennia, is that reversal in motion.

**The Queen is Gom, with amnesia** — the fragment left behind at the moment of dissolution. She inherited the world shortly after the Second War: blank, and infinitely powerful compared to everything around her. The Crystal Beings found her decades later, playing with Mud, and raised her — taught her everything she knows, without either party ever realizing what she actually was. The one thing she brought with her, the single surviving fragment of memory: a word. **Gomsdu** — which is, itself, the name of the land, "filled with the Energy of Gom, hence Gomsdu." Never just a description. The one thing she remembered.

**The True Union, if it happens:** the party reassembles the Shield. The Queen looks into the Black Stone and sees herself reflected — recognizes what she is, in the one object made from the same violence that made her forget. That's the actual Union: not Gom and Kulkrow, not the Queen and the Cloud — Gom recognizing Gom, mediated by an artifact that happens to be made of his brother. Kulkrow is nearly incidental to the mechanism. But if it happens, the fire dormant inside him ignites.

**Whether and when this occurs is genuinely in the players' hands** — not a scripted event on a fixed date, but a consequence of specific, deliberate action (reassembling and presenting the Shield) that may never happen at all in a given playthrough.

**Resolved — the actual trigger is minimal:** Queen and Black Stone together, in any circumstance. The mainstream Prophecy's staging (a specific place, a specific time, at the Pit) is the *ritual* version cultists believe is required — not the true mechanism. The other six Prophecy steps (Orks vs. the desert monsters, Nomads paving the way, Dwarves emptying the reservoirs, the Tree walking, the Crystal migrating, the Cloud) are all real, independently consequential events that can genuinely occur in play — just not gates on the true ending. **Design direction: the party may need to get every faction to actively pitch in**, which turns the endgame into something assembled through play rather than delivered on rails, and means different tables can see different subsets of the seven events actually happen.

## Possession: the Two Voices
Every lifeform has two voices inside them — Gom is a deafening shout, Kulkrow a soft whisper. A person can choose to give away more of themselves to Gom over time, incrementally, potentially losing all control of their own Self.

Full possession doesn't erase the mind — it erases control. The person stays completely aware, but their actions and speech belong entirely to Gom: a fully conscious passenger in a hijacked vehicle. The Men of Gom, and the Dwarven Chosen (see Factions), are both this end state.

**In game terms:** heavier Lightning investment grants real power, at the cost of the player's own control — potentially reaching a point where the game itself starts making a character's dialogue, combat, and travel decisions instead of the player. A genuine mechanical trade, not just flavor text.

**A second currency for the same investment:** since Gom exists in everyone's Blood, and Gom himself experiences time running backward (see "The True Union"), a character who taps deep enough into their own Lightning may receive glimpses of things that haven't happened to them yet — not full foresight, just leakage from a version of Gom that's already past this moment. Real incentive to risk the cost, beyond raw power.

## The Man of Gom — Tutorial Arc Template
*The bridge between story and mechanics. Same shape at all 4 starting locations, different local flavor each time.*

- The Man of Gom is not a separate being — it's **Gom himself**, present since Gom exists "in" everything in Gomsdu.
- Not obvious to the player: meeting them means the character gives up a small piece of themselves to Gom, not their entire Being — the Man of Gom has already made that full trade (see "Possession: the Two Voices," above). The terms of even that small piece are never disclosed.
- Before this point, the starting region is Form-only (Bane, for example) — this is why the tutorial feels restrictive.
- Gom becoming aware of the character is what grants Essence access and a dramatic AP increase (roughly 5x — e.g. 20 → 100 in Bane).
- **Trigger:** defeating a local Boss — a Cultist Sorcerer-equivalent who already has Essence — in a fight explicitly designed to require multiple players. Clearing it is what draws Gom's attention.
- **Confirmed:** this beat repeats identically in shape at all 4 starting locations, with different local flavor (different boss, different circumstances) each time.
- **Thematic note:** this makes the player mechanically complicit in the exact oppression dynamic that caused the First War — devotion and awareness traded for power, terms never disclosed — before they have any idea that dynamic exists. When the true history eventually surfaces, it recontextualizes the player's own character, not just the myth.
- **The Men of Gom are perpetually, visibly happy — this is Duping Delight**, the specific pleasure of successfully deceiving someone and getting away with it. Consistent with everything now established about Gom's character (see "The Pattern Underneath Both Wars"): every warm, welcoming moment with a Man of Gom is Gom privately relishing another successful con, running the same play on the player that he ran on Kulkrow for centuries. Play it completely straight in writing — the NPC should read as genuinely kind. The horror is only visible in hindsight.

### Bane: the Opening Sequence
The first working draft of what the template above actually looks like as a scene sequence — genuinely close to the very first session ever conceived for this game, before almost anything else existed.

1. **The Cell** — wake to alarms and smoke, the cultist attack already underway. First choice: force the door (loud) or work it quiet.
2. **The Cell Block** — chaos in the corridor, guards split between fighting cultists and fleeing. Room for a cellmate NPC to join; scattered items.
3. **The Yard** — first real fight-or-sneak decision against actual Cultists (Basic Cultist / Cultist Brute) — the first place Combat, the Sequencer, and the DM panel actually get exercised.
4. **The Burning Streets** — multiple paths through the city, an optional NPC to save or leave, environmental storytelling doing most of the work. One path leads to an optional **Abomination of Kulkrow** — an out-of-the-way mini-boss, something Cultists can conjure, not required to engage.
5. **The Priest** — encountered right before the Sorcerer, there specifically to demonstrate how magic actually works: the Sorcerer's instant-KO spells (Skull Explosion, Heart Detonation) land on everyone else, but not on him. First real, in-scene showcase of Focus/Essence functioning as defense, before it's ever explained.
6. **The Cultist Sorcerer** — the established boss, genuinely not soloable.
7. **The Man of Gom** — Essence unlocks, the AP jump, Separation taught — and he gets the party out of the burning city on horseback, closing the loop.

**Open, and worth deciding before building further:** whether this ships as a written scene script a DM triggers manually through the existing Combat/DM panels, or as an actual in-app Scene system (text, choices, item pickups, handing off to Combat and back). The first is buildable now. The second is a much bigger lift.

### Kulkrow's Awareness
The mirror to Gom's — and the actual reason every starting location has a Shield Fragment nearby, rather than that being a MacGuffin convenience.

Gom's attention is *earned*, through a decisive public act — defeat the Sorcerer, get noticed. **Kulkrow's attention is ambient — already there, waiting to be found.** He's fragmented across the Shield pieces and faintly present in everyone's Blood, the same way Gom is (see "Possession: the Two Voices"). Proximity to a Fragment lets him perceive whoever's nearby. His scattered body is his nervous system.

If a player figures out how to actually reach him — not yet mechanized, a real design target — he can assist them: information nobody else in Gomsdu has access to, a secret channel that exists because of what he already is, not because he was chosen for it. The shout demands to be noticed. The whisper is just already there.

### The First Spells & the Duality Irony
- The player's first taught spell, from the Man of Gom: **Separation** (Lava). Carves a piece of Gomsdu away from itself (e.g. a shovel-full of dirt); can be propelled telekinetically, but control is lost once thrown.
- The Bane Boss (the Kulkrow Sorcerer) uses two Lightning spells: **Skull Explosion** and **Heart Detonation** — both effectively instant-KO against a target with no Essence. Deliberately over-tuned for the Bane fight, but they target allied NPCs rather than players (plot armor). Focus is expected to become a universal stat, giving every character some baseline defense against effects like this.
- **Magic schools are domain-of-effect, not allegiance.** Lightning governs life/death effects; Lava governs matter/form effects. Anyone can reach for either — a Kulkrow-devoted Sorcerer casting Lightning, or Gom's own emissary teaching Lava, isn't a contradiction. Devotion shapes what a caster *wants* to do, not which flavor is technically available to them.
- **The irony this exposes:** Gom could never touch Matter directly at the dawn of creation — that was purely Kulkrow's domain, which is *why* the brothers needed each other to make the Living Stone at all. The Man of Gom teaching Separation means Gom's side has since acquired the very thing Gom originally lacked. Symmetrically, Kulkrow never achieved true Life/Death control — his own attempt (the Molten Ones) produced only mortality — yet his own Sorcerer wields instant-death Lightning. The official "jealousy" propaganda inverts in hindsight: it isn't that Kulkrow envied Gom's power and lost. Gom's side is the one that ended up encroaching into the other's original domain, while Kulkrow's side never got a stable equivalent.
- **The deeper point:** the Brothers are each permanently "all or nothing" — one whole domain each, none of the other, on their own. Their mortal followers aren't bound that way. A hybrid caster with real access to both schools is, in this one specific sense, more complete than either god they follow. Worth exploring further wherever duality comes up.

## Character Stats & Combat System

### Party Structure
A **Party** is one Player plus their own **Mini-Party** of 4 Characters — 5 total, run by that one Player. A **Meta-Party** is the full multiplayer table: up to 5 Players, each fielding their own 5-Character Party — 25 Characters in play at once across the whole table. Settled design, per creator, for roughly two weeks prior to this actually being written down (2026-08-19). Names above are working Dev names, deliberately kept separate from World/Wimbis vocabulary — not to be confused with `../language/gomsdu-glyphic-system.json`'s **ZINKARI**, the in-world Glyph for the same Self-plus-four shape, which stays a lore word rather than a UI term.

### Core Stats
Two headline stats, directly mirroring the creation myth — both are plain organizational headers now, not numbers in their own right:

- **Form** (from Kulkrow) — the body/matter side. Sub-stats:
  - **Power** — damage dealt per hit.
  - **Quickness** — determines where a character enters the round's countdown (the Global Counter). Higher Quickness = earlier entry.
  - **Range** — governs how wide a Block's effective timing window is (see Defense, below). Universal — anyone can Block, Range just determines how good they are at it. Build identity (Swift/Heavy/Parry) comes from how Quickness/Range/Power are allocated, not from locked class access.
- **Essence** (from Gom) — the magic/life side. Fully optional — a character can be almost entirely Form-based, with little to no Essence investment (a "pure martial" build). Sub-stats:
  - **Lightning** — Gom's school, life-magic.
  - **Lava** — Kulkrow's school, matter-magic.
  - **Focus** — casting potency, shared by both schools.
  - Both schools are common and valued purely for effectiveness in the wider world — most people don't know or care about their mythic origin. Some characters can start with real Lava affinity already. Whether schools can be freely mixed, or a character generally specializes in one, is still undecided.

**Health uses Wounds, not HP** — small numbers by design (Player builds: 7 flat across all three archetypes; basic mooks: 3–5; the Sorcerer boss: 12). Power is tier-based rather than archetype-based — both Player and the Sorcerer hit for 2, ordinary mooks hit for 1 — so a few solid hits actually matter, and build differentiation lives in timing/defense stats, not raw damage.

All playable races — "regular fantasy races" included — are ultimately Gom's experiments and, at base, separated Matter (Kulkrow's foundational contribution). Form + Essence applies universally, not just to Living Stone.

### Combat: the Global Counter
Not standard turn-based initiative. Every combatant commits their actions in advance (allocating AP across attacks, blocks, etc.), and the round resolves like this:

1. **The round's length = the highest total AP among all combatants that round.** A Global Counter starts at that number and ticks down to 0.
2. **Quickness sets each character's entry tick** — where they begin acting on the counter. Faster = enter higher (earlier).
3. **Once a character reaches their entry tick, they execute their committed actions in order.** Each action's duration in ticks equals its AP cost (a 10-AP attack takes 10 ticks; a 5-AP attack takes 5). The instant one action resolves, the next queued action begins at that same tick.
4. **A destroyed character's still-queued actions are cancelled.** An attack already in flight still resolves against a target that's since died — dealing "overkill," with no further effect. (Dead source cancels; dead target doesn't.)

**Worked example:** Player (20 AP) vs. Enemy A (10 AP) and Enemy B (10 AP). Player allocates 10→attack A, 5→attack A again, 5→block B. Enemy A spends all 10 on one attack; Enemy B splits 5/5 into two. Player enters at tick 20 (fastest); both enemies enter at tick 15. Player's first attack resolves at tick 10, destroying Enemy A and voiding Enemy A's own in-progress attack. Player's second attack lands as overkill at tick 5. Enemy B's attacks land at ticks 10 and 5 — the second is nullified by the Player's block.

**Design decision: enemies are DM-piloted, not automated.** For a multiplayer-first game, a human running the encounter (choosing enemy actions through the same staging interface players use) is more useful right now than building AI — it lets the actual flow get demo'd and tuned by hand before anyone commits to automated combat logic. Whoever hits Resolve — a player or the person running enemies — plays out the whole round at once, every staged action from every combatant interleaved by tick.

### Defense: Block, Maintain Block, Counter
Three tiers of the same idea, each buying more certainty at a cost — together forming a **Parry playstyle**, alongside Swift (many cheap attacks) and Heavy (few costly attacks):

- **Block** — a bet. Only works if the incoming attack resolves within your effective timing window, sized by **Range**.
- **Maintain Block** — costs a bit more AP; only available if you're faster than your attacker; holds the block for the entire round instead of betting on timing.
- **Counter** — an additional cost (stackable with or separate from Maintain) that turns a successful Block into an immediate counter-attack. (Was called "Deflect" earlier — renamed during the prototype build; same concept.)

### Still open
- Mixing Lightning and Lava freely, or generally specializing in one? Elves are now lore-confirmed hybrids (Lava for the Forest's features, Lightning for its behavior), so the tradition supports mixing as an elite/rare achievement — whether ordinary player characters can do the same freely, or whether it should stay exceptional, is still a build decision.
- Loyalty mechanic specifics for Kulkrow's forces
- Living Stone "approval" mechanic specifics
- The specific local boss/flavor for the Man of Gom beat at the other 3 starting locations
- Whether Range's timing-window and Maintain's speed-gate are actually enforced by the numbers, or left as narrative intent for now (the prototype doesn't check either yet)

## Numbers Are Relative, Not Absolute
A stat is a snapshot of relative positioning within whatever era's own scale is currently in play — never a fixed, cross-era measure of absolute power. This is why Gom and Kulkrow legitimately have "1" in everything at their origin (see Cosmology) despite being the two strongest beings the cosmology ever produces: they aren't weak, they're early. By the end of the Third Era, numbers everywhere are much larger — not because anyone got dramatically more powerful in absolute terms, but because Life itself has gotten more complex, more populated, more contested, and the math scales to match.

The corollary: **the Ledger's narrative text is what keeps the numbers honest**, not the numbers themselves. "1 wound" should read completely differently depending on when and where it lands — a shallow cut in Bane, a chunk torn from a lightning-body in the First War. The math stays small and clean; the fiction carries the actual sense of scale. Two different systems doing two different jobs, rather than one number trying to do both. Worth keeping in mind for every future stat, spell, or encounter — the target isn't a single universal balance point, it's era-appropriate relative weight.

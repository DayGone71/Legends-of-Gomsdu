## First Era Legend (seeded 2026-08-22)

The Map has 5 Personality clusters, mostly Living Stone: **Standers**
(Stand-Around-dominant), **Learners** (ask Gom questions), **Fighters**,
**Watchers** (follow Kulkrow), **Elites** (Marked by Gom). Gom and Kulkrow
both roam the Map — Kulkrow melts Tiles temporarily unenterable (the
existing blocked/traversable toggle, no new mechanic); Gom sometimes shapes
what Kulkrow carves into new Living Stone. No new art needed — First Era is
a barren ball of Iron, the existing Tile system already covers it; what's
missing is Interface (Facing, Active Character, Behaviors — mostly built).

### The Elite Path — earning the 4 Engravings (filled in 2026-08-26)

**All of this is WIP, changeable at any time — per creator, "I've spent a week thinking about it," not a locked spec.** The Elite capstone (Inert Stone → Living) in particular may end up mechanically different from what's written below; the one part expected to hold regardless is that it revolves around positioning and Hum together.

**Purpose, per creator: a tutorial to the World, Lore-heavy and Glyph-heavy.** The main thing it shows off is how Information affects Personality, which drives Behavior — and that Living Stone Behavior is confined to only performing things a Living Stone can actually conceive of, while the Player has access to the same Behaviors but can combine them in ways no Character native to Gomsdu can.

**Identification, tied directly to the Engravings themselves**: First Era baseline identification runs on KROW alone (any living/conscious being) — a Living Stone carrying any Engraving beyond that is, for the first time, using a real personal identifier rather than the bare category.

**Map layout**: Players spawn Southeast, next to Gom himself (he can teach up to 5 at once — a full Party). Kulkrow's Lava Fields are West. The last Totem of the reading path is North. The Standers are Central. The WM Stone are Northeast. The Fighting Stone are East. The retired Veteran is Southwest. The Duw Tiles are Northwest.

**Six paths, reachable in any order, each earning an Engraving:**
- **Learn to read** — Totems placed around the Map, each pointing (logically) to the next. The final Totem requires an unlikely Action — discoverable just by wandering up and trying things. Reading the chain in order to the end and performing that Action earns an Engraving.
- **Learn about Yom** — the more intelligent Living Stone ponder the Twilight Sky and what's beyond their reach (an early in-game exercise in how Ideas work). These Living Stone carry both the W and M Engravings ("WM Engraving") and use them to Hum; standing and facing another Hummer lets two Living Stone access Mindeye and trade Glyphs. Enough of these exchanges teaches the Player about Yom, the Star Father, earning an Engraving.
- **Fight the Fighters** — Living Stone who spar all day, earning Power and Resilience before testing themselves for real. A Hummer among them knows of a retired veteran; seeking him out teaches a Flow Technique. Separately, a 4x4 area flanked by Living Stone on every side — **the Gauntlet** — punishes anyone who doesn't already know what they're doing: wandering in draws an instant attack, and the Player has a round or two to leave before another Stone attacks. Beating a high-enough count of Gauntlet fighters earns an Engraving — Gom intervenes personally.
- **Outstand the Standers** — go with the Standers to Kulkrow's Lava Fields and stand still as Kulkrow approaches. Standing up to him grows Fear; crossing the Fear threshold triggers an automatic Flee. The real objective is minimizing that growth, not brute endurance — outlasting the other Standers earns an Engraving. The actual key: having already been introduced to Gomism by a WM-Engraved Living Stone, then simply holding the Gom Glyph in mind (KA, no movement) — thinking of Gom measurably reduces Fear.
- **Learn to Chisel** — Kulkrow's Lava Fields continuously regenerate: Molten Lava cools to Cooling Stone, which cools to Cooled Stone (the ordinary non-Iron Tiles). Cooling Stone is the briefest phase and the only one where Chiseling works. Chiseling itself repeats an effect from a Technique Kulkrow already knows, rescoped to the Living Stone Build as a Focus Power.
- **Duw's Secret** — tucked away: an odd Lava Flow sits on the Map, findable by wandering. Using KA together with an Idea and a Movement sequence (several Movements in a row) teaches the Duw Glyph itself — the game's own way of representing that Kulkrow knows the Glyphs, since this is the only means he has of reasonably expressing that.

**Two ways to earn each of the 3 directional Engravings, confirmed by creator**: South is taught by the WM Stone (from "Learn about Yom"), or awarded directly by Gom for following the Totems. West is taught by the Fighting Stone, or awarded by Gom for Chiseling. East is taught by the Standing Stone, or awarded by Gom for learning Duw. (Neither the Fighting Stone nor the Standing Stone has an English name yet, per creator — placeholder terms above.) Separately: **any Living Stone who already knows Chisel can Chisel an Engraving directly into another Living Stone**, bypassing both the Elites and Gom entirely.

**Elite status**: having all 4 Engravings. 1, 2, and 3 Engravings are just informal social standing — First Era Living Stone Society is described as "super informal," not a real ranked hierarchy below Elite. Elite itself is confirmed as "a hinge, a Status Symbol, and Gameplay" all at once: other Living Stone may keep a respectful distance from an Elite's adjacent Tiles, an Elite gains 3 Living Stone who stick with them as a Mini-Party, and real (if modest) stat buffs — "nothing huge, but made to make you feel like you won for sure."

**The Elite capstone**: manually turning an Inert Stone Living, "sort of." Needs the Mini-Party system (or genuine Multiplayer) to be real — the actual mechanism is 4 Characters standing in Tiles adjacent to the Inert Stone, correctly facing it, using WIM on it simultaneously. Won't happen by accident; needs either 4 real Player Characters or a single Elite's own Mini-Party. Per creator, this should only take a single Committed Plan Expression to coordinate once Behavior/Plan is far enough along.

**Gom's own presence on this Map**: every Player spawns close enough to reach him almost immediately — he occupies the Southeast Tile, communicates freely over Mindeye once a Player gets close, and also pushes them back away from him. He's meant to react to whatever a Player has actually learned/accomplished on the Map so far; otherwise he just suggests thinking about him when a Player has nothing else queued. He wanders the rest of the Map too — occasionally, for no stated reason, removing "the Current" from a Living Stone — and his own Behavior is deliberately built so he can always beat a Player back to the Southeast Tile (in Multiplayer, he only tracks the Host for this purpose).

*Not yet specified here: how the "first Chevron" (mentioned earlier as universal, given to every Stone once Gom finishes educating them) fits alongside these 3 earned directional Engravings toward the 4 Elite needs — worth confirming rather than assuming when this actually gets built.*

**The First War is a hidden history the Player secretly witnesses.** Since
the game is mostly played in the Third Era, having secretly been present at
the First War lets the Player Character know more than anyone around them —
a fourth-wall-adjacent device tied to the recurring "something unusual about
you" line.

**Trigger**: a specific interaction with an Inert Stone that no NPC's
Behavior math could ever produce — only the Player can do it, since Player
input was never routed through Behavior to begin with. Doing it starts the
War.

**Behavior control has 3 modes**: Player-direct (today's default);
Subconscious (Pursuit/Longing actually executing instead of just logging —
not built); Direct Control via Possession (Yom or another Character taking
over — always Possession under the hood, not a separate mode). A softer
Subconscious variant: weighting Information (Attachment/Desire) instead of
picking actions directly — the intended mechanism for time-skips ("let 13
hours pass, the Character fills it, with suggestions nudging them").

**Losing control is real gameplay stakes**: Mind can Break, Heart can
trigger fight-or-flight, Stomach can force an involuntary response —
governed by **Force**, an external pressure (a Message, a Script, another
Character's state) on a 1-7 scale. 7 = unsurmountable; 5-6 = likely but
resistible. Force shifts the target's Attachment/Desire/Longing, which
Pursuit already computes from — not a competing gauge. Other Players can
talk a struggling Character's Force back down, meant to make Reassuring
Psychologist and Cryptic Prophet real playstyles alongside Combat-focused
builds.

**Three new Personality Axes**, visible only via Yom's Direct Control:
**Hope** (Heart, shared by Gom and Kulkrow — Molten Ones inherit it, Living
Stone don't). **Anguish** (Head, Kulkrow only — its first threshold starts
the War, its second ends it). **Glee** (Head, Gom only — suppressed by
default; the War is when he stops suppressing it). All three act as Forces
on every Character in the Scene, including the Player.

**The climax is self-control, not combat.** Stand Around resists Force
better than any other action — struggling/running tires a Character and
weakens resistance; standing still holds Personality steady. Most Players
who start the War aren't expected to survive it — the win condition is
surviving long enough to witness Gom destroy the Molten Ones and Kulkrow
retreat.

**A worked Script for the War**: Gom's Messages coerce Living Stone into
attacking Molten Ones, who can only say `SUU` ("Stop") — a word Living
Stone can't even hear, since they only know engraved Glyphs. Molten Ones
are just Kulkrow, identical Personality, no individuation — Living Stone's
genuine independence is what makes the 5 Personality clusters above possible
at all. Finite Living Stone vs. infinite Molten Ones is a war of attrition
by design; Gom infusing himself into a Molten One near the end is what
creates the Rainbow Shards.

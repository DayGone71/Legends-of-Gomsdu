# Legends of Gomsdu — Dialogue Snippet Library

Companion to the Influences doc, scoped specifically to the Dialogue Engine.
Where Influences is pure resonance ("why this feels right"), this is working
notation — real, well-known lines decomposed into Set Pieces and Tags, kept
as a live proof that the grammar (`gomsdu-dialogue-mechanics.md`, and the
Call/Response section of `dialogue-system.md`) actually holds up against
language nobody wrote for this game. Meant to launch alongside the Dialogue
Interface itself, not sit as a side scratch file — add more as they come up,
same rule as Influences.

**Why this collection matters, on record:** across the first six quotes run
through this exercise, the same overlap surfaced four separate times,
completely unforced — two different Set-Piece combinations producing similar
surface text, two independently-run quotes landing on the same shape, one
quote's Call and Response turning out to be the literal same template with
one Noun swapped, and two people decomposing one famous line two valid,
different ways. Per dDd: that's the actual mechanism "amusement" is supposed
to run on once this is working well together — not authored jokes, emergent
overlap in a small grammar that recombines cleanly. Worth treating this
whole library as a running verification of that, not just a content bucket.

## Entries

**"The cake is a lie."** — an unattributed institutional message (Portal), to
whoever's still reading the walls.
`[Statement] ((cake)) ((lie))`
Two Nouns bridged by an implied "is" — the connector itself is flavor, not
structure.

---

**"This too shall pass."** — folk-attributed, to no one in particular.
`[Statement] ((this)) {shall pass}`
Simpler shape than the cake line — one Noun, one Verb, no second Noun needed
since nothing's being equated to anything else.

---

**"Life moves pretty fast."** — Ferris Bueller, to the audience.
`[Statement] ((Life)) {moves}`
"Pretty fast" — the intensity — isn't baked into the piece. It comes from
whoever's actually saying it: random chance, Personality, Relationship.
Same principle as the grammar never marking truth on its own; here it
doesn't mark intensity on its own either.

**"If you don't stop and look around once in a while, you could miss it."**
— same speech, same speaker, same addressee. **Both readings kept
deliberately** — this is the pair that first surfaced the overlap.
- dDd's read: `[Consequence] ((Noun)) {Verb}` + `[Command] ((Noun)) {Verb}`
  (Noun empty/carried over) + `[Consequence] ((Noun)) {Verb}`
- Claude's read: `[Condition] ((Noun)) {Verb}` + `[Consequence] ((Noun)) {Verb}`
  — reuses the existing ultimatum-family template directly, two pieces
  instead of three.

Both are legitimate decompositions of the identical sentence. **That's the
finding, not a discrepancy to resolve** — a `[Command]` and a `[Condition]`
don't carry the same Axes, so a Receiver inferring the wrong one from
similar surface text produces a genuinely different Chemistry outcome, not
just a vaguer one. Emergent, not curated, per dDd — this wasn't designed to
happen, it just did, on the first quote tried.

"Once in a while" (frequency) and "could" (certainty) are both hedge-words
handled the same way as "pretty fast" above — Slider-driven flavor,
candidates for Standard/Exchange-level Axes rather than anything tied to one
piece type specifically.

---

**"I only regret that I have but one life to lose for my country."** —
Nathan Hale (attributed), to his captors.
`[Stance] {regret}` + `[Statement] ((life)) ((country))`
"I only regret" is the wrapper, not the content — the same job `[Stance]`
already did in the very first Pool pass ("Considering the odds here, my
dear..."). "Only" hedges the Stance's own intensity, same family as "could"
and "once in a while" above. The core assertion underneath is the same
two-Noun `[Statement]` shape as the cake line, just bridged by "for" instead
of "is."

---

**"Sir, if you were my husband, I would poison your tea." / "Madam, if you
were my wife, I would drink it."** — Winston Churchill and Nancy Astor, to
each other (a widely told exchange, disputed as apocryphal — the Response is
the entire reason it survives).

Call: `[Address]` + `[Condition] ((husband))` + `[Consequence] ((tea))`
Response: `[Address]` + `[Condition] ((wife))` + `[Consequence]` *(Noun
carried over from the Call — same "it," never re-tagged)*

The Response isn't a fresh construction — it's the **literal same template**
as the Call, one Noun swapped (husband→wife), the payload flipped. Direct,
concrete proof that a template built for one line can carry a genuine
adversarial exchange, not just a single utterance — and a clean second
instance of Noun carry-over in the same session, first spotted in the
"stop and look around" `[Command]`.

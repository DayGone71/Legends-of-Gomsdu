# Legends of Gomsdu — Influences

A running list of pure references — not sources to adapt from, just touchstones. When a mechanic or a scene isn't landing, check it against these. The resonance is the point, not the plot.

---

**The Ark among the Philistines** (1 Samuel 5–6)

A captured, displaced object disorders everywhere it's set down — a rival god found broken and fallen before it, plague following through the streets. The people holding it become desperate to be rid of it and send it off as a test, uncertain whether what happens next will prove them right. The people who receive it do so with open, unguarded joy — sacrifice and celebration in the same breath. Carnage and homecoming, side by side, with no narrative signal for which reaction is the correct one.

*Resonance:* An outsider presence that the World reacts to without needing to understand it. Two completely different, equally valid reactions to the exact same thing, neither one commented on as more "right." Useful touchstone for how NPCs might respond to the Player.

---

**"The Funniest Joke in the World"** (Monty Python's Flying Circus)

A joke so lethal it becomes a classified military asset — translated one word at a time by soldiers who each learn only their single word so that none of them dies laughing, then deployed as an actual weapon. Every person involved treats the process with total bureaucratic solemnity. Nobody in the sketch is joking.

*Resonance:* A second flavor of Absurdity as Satire — not the Player/World perspective gap, but institutional seriousness applied without a flicker of irony to a premise that's inherently ridiculous. The comedy is the straight face, not a joke inside the straight face.

---

**Paramatman** (Vedantic philosophy)

In Vedantic thought, Paramatman is the Supreme Self — the singular, universal consciousness underlying every individual soul (Atman), rather than a separate being standing outside them. Individual selves aren't creations distinct from it; they're expressions or fragments of the same underlying ground.

*Resonance:* the principal influence behind the Gom/Kulkrow lore itself — referenced through Gomhead. Worth its own fuller writeup later, since it's foundational rather than incidental, but logged here so the connection doesn't get lost.

---

**Tata — found resonances**

Not designed in deliberately, but discovered afterward and too fitting to leave out. "Tata" (and close variants) means *Father* across a wide spread of unrelated languages, Sanskrit among them — which tracks, given the Bhagavad Gita's Krishna–Arjuna relationship as an ambient influence on the project. There's also the everyday English "ta-ta" — an informal goodbye (as in "ta-ta for now") — sitting right next to "Tata for now," the phrase used to step into the role.

*Resonance:* a name that means Father, Goodbye, and the fused Architect/Adversary all at once, unintentionally. Worth treating as confirmation rather than coincidence — the name was right before the reasons for it were fully known.
